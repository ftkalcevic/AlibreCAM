AlibreCAM Help

A CAM add-on for Alibre Design assemblies.

AlibreCAM adds a CAM tab to the Design Explorer of an assembly. In it you build a sequence of operations that machine the assembly's parts, generate a toolpath for each, see the toolpaths in the view, and post them as G-code for your machine. Toolpaths are 3+2 indexed: each Set Origin step can tilt or move the origin, and operations run under the origin above them.

The job is kept in a .cam file beside the assembly, with the same name. It's saved only when you press Save, and you're warned about unsaved changes when the assembly closes. All lengths are in millimetres, and Z is measured along the active origin's Z axis, pointing out of the material.

Press ? on the CAM tab's toolbar, or at the bottom left of a dialog, to open this page at the section for it.

CAM tab

The CAM tab is the add-on's home. Open it from the CAM command in an assembly. It holds a toolbar, the CAM tree, a status area, and the Refresh view and Save buttons. Toolpaths and origins are drawn in the view while the tab has focus, and hidden when it doesn't.

Toolbar

Draw over model
When pushed in, toolpaths and origins are drawn in front of the model, so you can always see them. When out, the model can hide them. On by default, and not saved.
Show toolpaths
Shows or hides every toolpath. Boundaries and picked feature outlines stay visible. On by default, and not saved.
Show stock
Shows or hides the stock's edges. On by default, and not saved.
Show origin
Shows or hides the active origin's axes and its plane. Turn it off when the axes lie over an edge you want to pick. On by default, and not saved.
? (Help)
Opens this page at the CAM tab section.
Settings (gear)
Opens the Settings dialog.

Tree

The tree has a fixed Setup at the top and the Operations sequence below it. Operations run from top to bottom. Drag a step to reorder it. Each node has an image: a gear for Setup, a machine, tools, a block of material and a wireframe stock; axes for a Set Origin and a folder for a folder; each tool shows its type (flat end mill, ball mill, corner radius mill, V-mill, chamfer mill, taper mill, thread mill or drill); and each kind of operation has its own. The images grow with the CAM View Size setting. A red exclamation mark over a node's image means it has a problem: an operation with no toolpath (never generated, cleared, changed since, or Generate failed), a Set Origin that isn't complete, the machine when its file can't be found or read, the material when it's no longer in the list, or the stock when it can't be read. Folders are never marked, and suppressed operations aren't, as they aren't generated on purpose.

Setup ▸ Machine
The machine the job is cut on, shown as Machine - name. Double-click it, or right-click and Edit Machine, to open the Machine dialog.
Setup ▸ Tools
The machine's tools. Click a tool to select it: new operations start with the selected tool. Double-click Tools or a tool to open the Machine dialog at its Tools tab.
Setup ▸ Material
The material the job is cut from, shown as Material - name. Right-click it to choose a material, or No Material, or Edit Materials to change the list. The Feeds & Speeds tab's From Tool button uses it. Double-click opens the Materials tab.
Setup ▸ Stock
The material the parts are cut from. A job has at most one stock, and the node shows its kind, such as Stock - Box Stock. Right-click it to make one: Box Stock, Part Box Stock or Import Stock. Once there's a stock, those are greyed, and Edit (or double-clicking Stock) changes it and Delete removes it. The stock is drawn as its edges in teal, and it's measured along the assembly's axes.
Operations
The ordered sequence of Set Origin steps, folders and operations. Right-click it to add steps, generate all toolpaths, or post.
Set Origin
Defines the origin (work coordinate frame) for the steps below it, up to the next Set Origin. The first one that isn't suppressed is the work zero for posting. Selecting one draws it in the view.
Folder
A group of operations, which can be generated or posted together. Folders don't nest. Use Rename to change a folder's name.
Operation
A machining operation: Drill, Hole Pocketing, Facing, Profiling or Pocketing. Double-click it, or use Edit, to open the Operation dialog. Selecting an operation draws its toolpath in bold and its picked geometry in orange.
Choosing several operations
Ctrl+click operations to choose several, even in different folders; Ctrl+click a chosen one again to take it off. Shift+click chooses every operation between the last one clicked and this one. A plain click goes back to one. Right-click a chosen operation for Edit N Operations, which edits them together (see the Operation dialog), Generate, which generates them all, Clear Toolpaths, or Simulate. Double-clicking a chosen one edits them together too.
Suppressed items
Shown grey, with their toolpaths hidden (they're kept, and show again when unsuppressed). A suppressed Set Origin is skipped, and suppressed operations are left out of Generate and Post.

Opens the step's dialog.

Edits the item's name in place in the tree.
Duplicate
Adds a copy of the step below it.
Suppress
Turns the step off, or back on. See Suppressed items above.

Removes the step (a folder with its operations).
Generate
Calculates the toolpaths of the chosen operation, folder, or every operation. Problems are reported in the status area. If it takes more than half a second, the Generating window shows how it's going.

Status and buttons

Status
Messages from the last action, such as Generate's results and problems.
Refresh view
Draws the toolpaths and origin again. Use it after a native Alibre command, such as Measure, has ended the CAM display.

Generating window

Appears when Generate (from the tree, a folder, several chosen operations, or an operation dialog's Generate button) takes more than half a second, in the middle of Alibre's window, and closes when it's done. It shows the operation being generated (and which of how many), what it's doing (reading the area, reading the parts, each level of a waterline operation, dropping the tool on the parts, moving points onto the true surface), and a bar for how far along that is, where that's known. Alibre is busy while it's open, so clicks and keys wait until Generate ends; there's no Cancel. It has no ? button for the same reason.

Simulation

Plays the generated toolpaths against the stock in Alibre's view, cutting it a move at a time. The stock is a grid of small cubes (cells) over its box, shown shaded in place of its outline; a cell is cut away when its centre falls inside the tool. Each tool cuts with its own shape: flat, ball, corner radius, V, chamfer, taper and drill ends (a thread mill as a plain cylinder). The tool is drawn in yellow where it is. Operations run in the sequence's order, each in its own Set Origin's direction, so tilted origins cut along their tool axis. The holder isn't modelled, and nothing is checked against the parts yet. Open it with Simulate on the CAM tab's right-click menus; it needs a stock, and operations with no toolpath, origin or tool are left out (the dialog says which).

Position
The operation being cut, and the move of all the moves. Arcs are split into short straight moves.
Scrub bar
Drag to any point in the program. When you let go, the stock is cut to there (going back starts again from the whole stock, which can take a moment).
Rewind
Back to the start, with the stock whole again.
Step
Cuts one move.
Play / Pause
Cuts moves continuously at the speed set, redrawing as it goes, until the end or a warning.
To Op End
Cuts to the end of the current operation.
To End
Cuts everything that's left.
Speed
How many moves are cut at each step while playing, from 1 to 500.
Auto divide voxels
On (the default), solid and empty parts of the stock are kept whole, and are divided into small cells only where the tool cuts them. That lets the cells be fine, about 0.05 mm on a typical part, while starting quickly. Turn it off to choose the resolution yourself with the slider. Changing it starts the stock again and cuts back to the same move. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Resolution
Only while Auto divide voxels is off. From Fine (small cells: sharper, slower, more memory) to Coarse (large cells: blockier, faster). The choices depend on the stock's size, with Medium in the middle as the start. Letting go at a new stop starts the stock again and cuts back to the same move. Remembered until Alibre closes.
Warning
A rapid move that would cut stock stops the simulation and is drawn in thick red, naming the operation and its toolpath move. Play, Step or the other buttons carry on from there.
Show
What's drawn in the view. Each is remembered until Alibre closes.
  • Stock: the cut stock, shaded (on at first).
  • Part: the parts as the simulation sees them, in green: every visible, unsuppressed part in the assembly, in the same cells as the stock, so you can check they line up with the model. An imported stock's own part is left out. The parts are read the first time any of the last three boxes is ticked, which can take a moment.
  • Not enough removed: stock still left outside the parts, in amber. Stock to Leave shows here too.
  • Too much removed: part material the simulation has cut away, in red, drawn over the model so it shows inside the part.
Differences within one cell of a part's surface aren't shown, so the surface doesn't speckle. The line under the boxes gives each difference's volume in mm³, worked out again as the stock is cut, or says why the parts couldn't be read.
Save STL...
Saves the cut stock as it is at the current move as an STL file, in millimetres and in the assembly's coordinates, so it lines up with the parts. The first suggestion is <job> - simulation.stl beside the .cam file, then the last folder used. The surface is the cells' surface, so it's blocky, with steps the size of a cell; large and small faces meet with joins that some programs call not watertight, though viewers and slicers open it. The tool isn't saved. The line under the controls says what was saved.
Close
Ends the simulation. The stock is drawn as its outline again.

Machine

The machine the job is cut on, its tools, and the list of materials. Each machine is a file of its own in %APPDATA%\AlibreCAM\machines\, holding its settings and its tools, and the job remembers which one it uses. A new job starts on the machine used last. Changes are saved straight away. Open it by double-clicking Machine, Tools or Material on the CAM tab.

Machine
The machine this job uses. Choosing another moves the job to it, and it becomes the machine new jobs start on.
New
Makes a new machine with no tools, and moves the job to it.
Copy
Makes a copy of the machine, with its tools, under a new name, and moves the job to it.
Delete
Deletes the machine's file and its tools, after asking. The job moves to another machine. The only machine can't be deleted.
Name
The machine's name, shown in the tree as Machine - name.
Number of axes
3, or 4 for a machine with a rotary axis.
Rotary axis
Which way the rotary (4th) axis runs: along -X, +X, -Y or +Y. Only used with 4 axes. Not used for toolpaths yet.
Travel X, Y, Z
How far the machine can move along each axis, in millimetres. Each must be more than 0. Not used for checking toolpaths yet.
Max spindle speed
The spindle's top speed, in RPM. It must be more than 0. Not used for checking speeds yet.
Close
Closes the dialog. Everything is already saved.

Number
The tool number (T number) the machine knows the tool by. It's written at tool changes.

A description of the tool, shown in lists and written in the program.
Units
The unit of the tool's sizes. Each tool keeps its own unit, so an imperial cutter keeps its exact size in a metric job.
Diameter
The cutting diameter.
Flute length
The length of the cutting part.
Overall length
The tool's length out of the holder.
Flute count
The number of flutes. Every type but drills.
Corner radius
Corner radius mills: the radius at the end's corner, no more than half the diameter.
Tip diameter
V-mills, chamfer mills and taper mills: the diameter at the tip, 0 for a sharp point. It must be less than the diameter.
Included angle (deg)

Pitch

Taper angle (deg)

Thread angle (deg)

Point angle (deg)
The drill's included point angle, such as 118. Used by the Drilling tab's Add drill tip to depth.

Set Origin

Defines an origin from picked geometry or Alibre's design planes, axes and points: its Z axis (the tool axis, pointing out of the material), its position, and its X direction. The steps below it, up to the next Set Origin, run in this origin. The origin is drawn with red, green and blue axes and its XY plane as an outline and grid.

Name
The step's name in the tree.
Plane (Z) Pick
Picks the geometry that gives Z. A new Set Origin starts with nothing picked: click Pick, then click in the view. A flat face points Z out of the material, a cylindrical face gives its axis, a circular edge gives its axis and its centre, and a straight edge runs Z along it. A design plane, such as Alibre's XY plane, points Z along its normal, and a design axis runs Z along it; use Flip Z if it points the wrong way.
Position Pick
Picks an edge or a design point for the origin's position: the nearer end of a straight edge, a circle's centre, or the point, such as Alibre's Origin. Without one, the plane pick gives the position when it's an edge.
Position Clear
Removes the position pick.
X direction Pick
Picks a straight edge for X, projected onto the XY plane and pointing away from the end nearer where you clicked, or a design axis, pointing along the axis. Without one, X is the assembly's X projected onto the plane (or its Y, if X is too close to the tool axis).
X direction Clear
Removes the X direction pick.
Flip Z
Reverses the Z axis.
Turn X about Z
Turns X about Z in quarter turns.
Offset X, Y, Z
Moves the origin along its own axes, in millimetres, after everything else.
Prompt and result
What to pick next, and the resulting origin or why it can't be made.
OK
Saves the origin.
Cancel
Closes the dialog without changing the step.

What's being posted: every operation, or a folder's.
Post
The post to use, such as LinuxCNC. The built-in APT CL data post is last. The dialog starts on the post used last time.
Output file
Where the program is written. It's suggested beside the .cam file, named after the assembly, with the post's extension.
Browse
Chooses the output file.
Post (button)
Writes the program.
Cancel
Closes the dialog without posting.

Operation dialog

The settings of one operation, on tabs. The dialog stays open while you work in the view, so you can pick geometry. Every operation has Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds and Clearance tabs, followed by its own:

  • Drill: Drilling.
  • Hole Pocketing: Hole Pocketing.
  • Facing: Cut Levels, Entry/Exit, Facing.
  • Profiling: Cut Levels, Entry/Exit, Profiling.
  • Pocketing: Cut Levels, Entry/Exit, Pocketing.
  • Chamfer: Entry/Exit, Chamfering. It has no Cut Levels tab: its depth comes from the chamfer.
  • Engrave: Cut Levels, Entry/Exit, Engraving.

Pressing ? opens the help for the tab that's showing.

Editing several operations together. With several operations chosen in the tree, the dialog shows only the tabs they all have: Tool, Feeds & Speeds and Clearance; Cut Levels and Entry/Exit when they're all Facing, Profiling or Pocketing; and their own tab when they're all the same kind. Features aren't shown, as each operation's geometry is its own. A box that's empty, a group with no choice, or a check box neither on nor off means the operations differ there. Only the fields you change are written to every operation; everything else keeps each operation's own value. If a change doesn't suit an operation, OK names it and nothing is changed. Name and Generate aren't available, and Pick and Locate work only when the operations all run under the same Set Origin.

Name
The operation's name in the tree.
Generate
Applies the dialog's settings to the job and generates this operation's toolpath, leaving the dialog open.
OK
Applies the settings and closes the dialog. Changing settings discards the old toolpath.
Cancel
Closes the dialog without changing the operation.

Features tab

The geometry the operation machines, in the order it's machined. Each operation takes its own kinds of geometry: Drill and Hole Pocketing take circles, arcs, hole faces and design points; Facing, Profiling and Pocketing take flat faces and edges, including spline curves; Plunge Roughing, Waterline Roughing, Waterline Finishing and Parallel Finishing take any faces, curved ones too, and edges. For the 2 1/2 axis operations only the geometry's position in the origin's XY plane matters: the heights come from the Cut Levels tab. Plunge Roughing and Waterline Roughing project their geometry onto the origin's XY plane to make the area they rough; their depths come from the parts. Each face is an area of its own, and edges whose ends meet are grouped into one.

Feature list
Each feature's type, part, diameter, and X, Y and Z in the operation's origin. Drag rows to reorder them. Choosing rows selects them in the view. Press Delete to remove the chosen rows.
Add the stock box's footprint
For area operations (Facing, Profiling, Pocketing, Chamfer, Engrave and Plunge Roughing; not Drill or Hole Pocketing). Adds the stock's box, seen from above along the origin's Z, to the area, as well as anything in the list, which can then be empty. It's a rectangle, or wider where the origin is tilted. For an imported stock part, it's the box round the part. Facing, Profiling, Pocketing, Chamfer and Engrave take it as an area of its own, after the listed ones, at the stock's top, so the cut geometry's level (where Cut Levels' At Top and At Bottom are measured from) is at least the stock's top. Plunge Roughing merges it with the rest of its area, and as nothing is beyond the stock's edges, its plunges reach them. Generate stops if there's no stock.
Select Features
Turns the view's selection into the list. The listed features start out selected; click or Ctrl+click in the view to add or remove features, and press Done when finished. New features are added at the end.
Delete
Removes the chosen rows.
Note
Says what this operation accepts, and any problem with the list.
Sort ▸ Order
How Sort orders the list: Minimum distance (from the first feature, always to the nearest one left), or Directional (zigzag rows, X first or Y first).
Sort ▸ Start corner
For Directional, the corner the rows start from: top left, top right, bottom left or bottom right.
Sort
Reorders the list now.

Sorting tab

Cut Levels tab

The heights an area is cut at, for Facing, Profiling and Pocketing. The cut geometry's height is the highest point of all the selected features, unless you pick a top. The depth is split into rough levels, then finish levels.

At Top
The cut starts at the selected geometry's height and goes the total depth down.
At Bottom
The cut ends at the selected geometry's height, starting the total depth above it.
Pick Top
The cut starts at the Z in the box. Pick fills it with a picked item's Z in the operation's origin.
Total Cut Depth
How deep the cut is in all. Pick sets it to a picked item's distance from the cut geometry. A total of 0 cuts one pass at the top.
Rough Depth and Finish Depth
How the total is shared between roughing and finishing; they always add up to the total. The slider sets the rough share.
Rough Depth/Cut
The most each rough level cuts. The levels are spaced evenly. The slider sets it as a percentage of the rough depth.
Finish Depth/Cut
The most each finish level cuts, likewise.
Clear Island Tops
To be completed. Not used yet.
Use 3D Model to Detect Depth
To be completed. Not used yet.
Depth First
Each area, profile or pocket is cut to its full depth before the next.
Level First
Every area is cut at a level before going down to the next level.

Entry/Exit tab

How the tool moves into and out of each cut, for Facing, Profiling and Pocketing. A positive angle or radius puts the lead on the side away from the geometry, and a negative one on the other side. Leads run at the cut feed.

Entry Motions: None
The tool plunges straight down at the start of the cut.
Entry Motions: 2D Entry
The tool goes down beside the cut and leads in at the cut's level, as set in Engage Motion.
Entry Motions: Along 3D Path

Plunge Roughing tab

Plunge Roughing roughs out a 3D shape by feeding the tool straight down (along the origin's -Z) again and again, cutting out round plugs. The area comes from the Features tab, projected onto the origin's XY plane (seen from above): each face covers its whole footprint, with any holes inside it filled, and each group of edges whose ends meet must make a closed loop, which covers its inside. Faces and loops that touch or overlap merge into one area. The area is worked out on a fine grid, so its edge is accurate to a fraction of a millimetre. It's filled with plunges packed as circles are: rows along the Angle of Cuts, alternate rows shifted half a stepover, with the grid centred in the area. With a stepover of 100% or more, no two plunges overlap: rows are exactly the packing distance apart, and a row's end only gets an extra plunge where it's at least a tool diameter from every other plunge. Under 100% the plunges overlap anyway, so the rows are spread to reach the area's edges, and each row's ends get a plunge where the grid leaves a gap. Where a part carries on past the area's edge, such as a pocket's walls, the whole tool plus the Stock to Leave stays inside the area, so the wall isn't cut. Where nothing of any part is beyond the edge, as at the part's own outside edge, the edge is open and the tool's centre goes right up to it, so the stock round the part is roughed too. (If the parts can't be read, every edge is treated as a wall.) Each plunge goes down until the tool, anywhere across its width, would come within the Stock to Leave of a part, so it follows the 3D shape below; where no part is under it, it goes to the stock's bottom. Every visible, unsuppressed part in the assembly counts (an imported stock's own part doesn't). Any mill or drill can be used; the depth is worked out for its shape. Plunges with nothing to cut are left out. Its tabs are Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds, Clearance and this one.

Stock to Leave
Material left on the parts, and on the area's walls, for later operations. 0 or more.
Path Direction: Zig / Zig Zag
The order along each row. Zig goes the same way along every row; Zig Zag comes back along the next.
Start Side: Bottom / Top
Which side the first row is on: the origin's -Y side (Bottom) or +Y side (Top), as Pocketing's Linear Cuts do. With rows along Y, Bottom is the -X side.
Angle of Cuts
The direction the rows run, the path each row's plunges are traced along: 90 runs along the origin's X, 0 along its Y.
Step Over Control
How far apart neighbouring plunges are, as a % of the tool diameter or a distance; rows are this times 0.87 apart. It has no maximum. To leave no plugs between plunges it should be at most about 87% of the diameter; more than that leaves plugs standing between them. 75% is the default.
Cut Engage/Retract: Approach

It comes back up there at the retract feed, and moves between plunges at that height. It starts and ends at the clearance plane, which must be at or above this height.

Waterline Roughing tab

Waterline Roughing roughs out a 3D shape in horizontal slices, from the top of the cut down to its bottom (see its Cut Levels tab). Its tabs are Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds, Clearance, Cut Levels and this one.

The area comes from the Features tab as for Plunge Roughing: picked faces and closed loops of edges (and the stock box's footprint if ticked) seen from above and merged. At each level the tool's centre may go anywhere in the area that's at least the tool's radius plus the Stock to Leave from any part that comes within the Stock to Leave of the level, so the parts stand as islands with the Stock to Leave on their tops and sides. Where no part is beyond the area's edge, the tool's centre goes right up to the edge (and past it by the Linear Extension). A level is entered at the plunge feed from 1 mm above the level before (the first from 1 mm above the top). The tool stays down from one pass to the next when the move is short and stays in the cleared area, and otherwise lifts to the clearance plane, which must be at least 1 mm above the top. The tool's true shape is used (a ball mill's ball, a corner radius, a V's point), so a curved or sloping part is roughed as closely as the tool can reach. Everything is worked out on a fine grid, so the passes are accurate to a fraction of a millimetre.

Stock to Leave
Material left covering the parts, on their tops and their sides: the tool's bottom stays this far above them, and its side this far from them. It also raises each flat face's level by this much. 0 or more.
Linear Extension
How far past the area's edge the tool's centre travels where the edge is open (no part beyond it), for every pattern, so the edge is cut cleanly. Parts still keep the tool its radius plus the Stock to Leave away. 0 (the default) stops the centre on the edge.
Cut Pattern: Part Offset
Loops round the parts at each level, the first the Stock to Leave off them, each further one a stepover further out, cut off at the area's edge. Where a level has no part in it, Stock Offset is used.
Cut Pattern: Stock Offset
Loops in from the area's (the stock's) edge, each a stepover further in, cut off where they reach the parts, then a loop round the parts at the Stock to Leave.
Cut Pattern: Linear
Straight rows along the Angle of Cuts, a stepover apart, cut off at the parts and the area's edge, then a lap round the edge of the cleared area.
Cut Direction
Climb keeps the uncut material on the tool's right (clockwise spindle), Conventional on its left. Mixed: the offset patterns alternate direction from one set of loops to the next (an offset cut off at an edge may be cut from either end), and Linear rows zigzag; the loop round the parts and the final lap are always cut climb.
Offset: Pocket Start Point
For Part Offset and Stock Offset. Inside starts nearest the parts and works outwards; Outside (the default) starts at the stock's edge and works in.
Linear: Start at Bottom / Start at Top
For Linear. Start the rows at the origin's -Y side or +Y side, as Pocketing's Linear Cuts do.
Linear: Angle of Cuts
For Linear: the rows' direction. 90 runs along the origin's X, 0 along its Y.
Stepover: % Tool Dia / Distance / Scallop
How far apart the loops or rows are: a percentage of the tool's diameter (at most 100%; 50% is the default), a distance, or from the scallop height, the ridge left between two passes. Waterline Finishing tab

Waterline Finishing finishes the sides of a 3D shape with a single lap round the parts at each level, from the top of the cut down to its bottom (see its Cut Levels tab, which it shares with Waterline Roughing). Its tabs are Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds, Clearance, Cut Levels and this one. It's meant to follow a roughing operation, with a ball mill for curved shapes.

The area comes from the Features tab as for Waterline Roughing. At each level the lap is where the tool, with its true shape, just touches the parts (less the Stock to Leave), and is cut off at the area's edge, or past it by the Overtravel. Each separate part (island) gets its own lap. Where the parts slope gently, so that two levels' laps are further apart than the stepover, extra levels are added between them until no gap is wider than the stepover. A lap is entered at rapid to the Approach Distance above its start, then at the plunge feed. The tool stays down from one lap to the next lap round the same part when the move is short and clear of the parts, and otherwise lifts to the clearance plane.

The laps are found on a fine grid first, then every point is moved sideways onto the level exactly, with the tool's shape dropped onto the parts' facets, and with a ball mill onto the faces' true surfaces (as Parallel Finishing's rows are: spheres, cylinders, cones, tori and splines quickly by AlibreCAM, any other face more slowly through Alibre). Where the level line itself doubles back in a thin finger or a tiny loop (under 0.2 mm wide and 3 mm long, as where the tool just grazes a slight ridge), the lap goes straight across instead, and tiny steps backwards are left out, but only where the tool keeps clear of the part doing so. Then they're simplified: a straight move may replace several points if it stays within 0.01 mm of them sideways, or if the tool's height along it stays within 0.01 mm of the level (and it's within 0.5 mm of them). So on steep sides the laps follow the parts closely, and on shallow ground, where a tiny difference in height moves the level line a long way sideways, they run smoothly instead of wandering.

The laps finish walls only: on a flat face they run round its edge but don't cover it. Tick Finish Flats to clear the flats as well.

Stock to Leave
Material left covering the parts: the tool stays this far from them. 0 (the default) cuts to the parts' surface. 0 or more.
Overtravel
How far past the area's edge the tool's centre travels, as Parallel Finishing's does, so the edge is finished cleanly. 0 (the default) stops the centre on the edge. The parts are always kept clear.
Approach Distance
Coming down to start a cut, the tool moves at rapid to this far above where the cut starts, then at the plunge feed the rest of the way, rather than feeding down slowly through what roughing has already cleared. 2 mm by default; 0 or more. It's measured from the start of each cut, so make sure the roughing has cleared at least this far above it.
Cut Direction
Climb (the default) keeps the part on the tool's right (clockwise spindle), so each lap goes clockwise round the part from above; Conventional goes anticlockwise. Mixed alternates from one level to the next.
Finish Flats
Off by default. When ticked, each up-facing flat face of the parts in the area is also cleared at its height (plus the Stock to Leave), after the laps round its walls: loops a stepover apart from the flat's edge inwards, wherever the tool's tip rests on the flat, so the tool never cuts into the walls or anything standing on the flat. A level is added at each flat's height even with Clear Flats off on the Cut Levels tab. The loops follow the Cut Direction (Mixed alternates loop by loop) and the Stepover, so a ball mill's Scallop stepover gives the same scallop on the flats.
Stepover: Optimise Stepover
In the Stepover group; the group's other choices can only be changed with it ticked. Off (the default): the levels are the Cut Levels tab's stepdown only (and Clear Flats' levels, if it's ticked). Ticked: the levels are chosen to suit the slope: from each level, the next stepdown level is tried, and the step halved (to no less than 0.02 mm) until the laps there are no further across than the stepover from where the tool reached at the level above. Steep sides keep the whole stepdown; shallow ones get more levels. The levels are shared by the whole area, so a shallow patch anywhere adds levels everywhere at those heights.
Stepover: % Tool Dia / Distance / Scallop
Used to add levels when Optimise Stepover is ticked, and as the spacing of Finish Flats' passes (which use it as set, even while it's greyed). The widest the gap between two neighbouring laps may be, measured across, before an extra level is added between them. It matters where the parts' sides are shallow; on steep sides the stepdown sets the spacing. A percentage of the tool's diameter (10% by default), a distance (0.5 mm by default), or from the scallop height, the ridge left between two laps: 2 × √(h(2r − h)) for the tool's corner radius r (a ball mill's radius). A flat end mill leaves no scallop, so it uses 90% of its diameter.

Parallel Finishing tab

Parallel Finishing finishes a 3D shape with straight rows a stepover apart, the tool rising and falling to follow the parts' surface with its own shape (a ball mill's ball, a corner radius, a flat end), so it never cuts into them. It suits gently curved and sloping surfaces; on steep walls the rows are far apart along the wall, so use Waterline Finishing there. Its tabs are Features, Tool, Feeds & Speeds, Clearance, Cut Levels (the waterline operations' tab, showing only Top and Bottom) and this one.

The area comes from the Features tab as for the waterline operations. The tool's centre runs right up to the area's edge, and past it by the Overtravel. Where there's no part under the tool, it goes down to the bottom of the cut. It's always a single finishing pass over the whole depth: its Cut Levels tab has only Top and Bottom. Rows are cut only where the surface is below the Top, and go no lower than the Bottom. Each row is entered at rapid to the Approach Distance above its start, then at the plunge feed. The tool's height along each row is worked out exactly for its shape against the parts' triangles (the facets Alibre gives for each part), checked every half grid cell along the row (the tool's diameter / 80) and kept to within 0.01 mm when the moves are simplified. The facets are flat, so on a curved surface they're a little off the true surface. With a ball mill each point is then moved onto the true surface of the face it touches, so the ball sits exactly its radius plus the Stock to Leave from it. Spheres, cylinders, cones, tori and spline (NURBS) faces are worked out by AlibreCAM itself, on all the computer's cores, which is quick; a face whose shape can't be read that way is asked of Alibre a point at a time, which is much slower. Flat faces are already exact, so points on them are left as they are. To save time, every second point along a row is moved onto the surface, and each point between two on the same face takes the middle of their heights, which is within a micron or so on a smooth surface. Points where it can't be done (the contact is on the ball's side, or Alibre can't say) keep the facets' answer; the log says how many and why. Alibre has to answer for every point one at a time, so with a ball mill Generate can take tens of seconds, and Alibre is busy until it's done.
